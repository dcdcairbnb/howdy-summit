Best Apres-Ski in Summit County

Apres-ski is the county's signature ritual: the lifts stop spinning around 4pm, you walk in still wearing your boots, and you order before you have taken your gear off. Every resort base has its own version, and the best ones are a hundred feet from where you click out of your bindings.

The spots worth planning around:

  • T-Bar (Peak 8 base, Breckenridge). Ski boots welcome, DJs on weekends, the loudest apres in Breck. Fills the second the lifts close.
  • Copper Top Bar & Grill (Peak 9, Breckenridge). Beaver Run's slopeside deck. Big drinks, big sun, easy ski-in access.
  • Kickapoo Tavern (Keystone). Steps from the River Run gondola. The default Keystone round, and it handles large groups.
  • 9280' Tap House (Keystone). River Run taproom named for the elevation. Colorado beer and fire-pit patio seating.
  • Ten Mile Tavern (Copper Mountain). Center Village tavern with live music, a big slopeside deck, and the whole Copper apres crowd.
  • Jack's Slopeside Grill and Downhill Duke's (Copper Mountain). Ski off American Flyer and sit down. Burgers, beer, dog-friendly patios.
  • 6th Alley Bar & Grill (Arapahoe Basin). Beers on the deck at 10,780 feet staring straight up at the East Wall.
  • The Beach at Arapahoe Basin. A-Basin's legendary parking-lot tailgate. Grills, lawn chairs, spring corn snow, free to join, bring your own.
  • The Goat Soup & Whiskey Tavern (Keystone). Cheap beer, whiskey, and live music. Where the lifties actually drink.
  • Mi Casa Mexican Restaurant & Cantina (Breckenridge). The margarita landmark since 1980. Happy hour on the deck is a rite of passage.

One warning: alcohol hits roughly twice as hard at 9,600 feet. Drink water between rounds, especially on night one.

Main Street Breckenridge

Main Street Breckenridge is the strip. Roughly a mile of restored 1880s Victorian storefronts from South Main to North Main, with Peak 8 rising straight behind them, and it holds the densest run of bars, restaurants, and shops in the county. It is all walkable, and it is worth doing once from end to end.

The classics: Gold Pan Saloon (one of the oldest continuously operating bars west of the Mississippi, original back bar intact), Burke & Riley's Irish Pub (upstairs balcony over the street), Napper Tandy's, RMU Tavern (run by the Rocky Mountain Underground ski builders, dogs welcome), Blue Stag Saloon (elk, bison, wood-fired pizza, fire pits out back), Downstairs at Eric's (basement sports bar and arcade, 100-plus beers, family-friendly early), and Cecilia's Martini Bar, which stays open after everything else in town has closed.

Decks and rooftops stack the strip too: Fatty's Pizzeria on Ridge Street, South Ridge Seafood Grill, and The Canteen Taphouse & Tavern on North Main. For tasting rooms without leaving the sidewalk, there is the Breckenridge Distillery Main Street Tasting Room, Mythology Distillery in the historic barn on North Main, Continental Divide Winery, Steep Brewing & Coffee Co., and the Broken Compass Main Street taproom. Eat at Empire Burger, Fatty's, Crepes a la Cart on the walk home, or book ahead at Hearthstone, Briar Rose Chophouse, Modis, Rootstalk, or Aurum Food & Wine.

Do not fight for a parking space. Park free in the F-Lot and ride the free BreckConnect gondola or the free Breckenridge Free Ride bus into town.

The Six Summit County Towns

Breckenridge

An 1859 gold-rush town that never got torn down. One of Colorado's largest National Historic Districts, five ski peaks straight above it, and the most nightlife, restaurants, and shopping in the county. Busiest and priciest, and worth it once. Don't miss the free BreckConnect Gondola, Country Boy Mine (walk 1,000 feet into an 1887 gold mine), the Breckenridge Distillery, Broken Compass Brewing, and the free sledding hill at Carter Park.

Frisco

The geographic middle of the county, right where I-70 meets Highway 9. A low-key Main Street of locally owned restaurants and shops, a marina on the west side of Dillon Reservoir, and the best value for a group that wants to split time between resorts. Essentials: Butterhorn Bakery & Cafe (the county's breakfast benchmark, no reservations, get there before 9am), Outer Range Brewing Co. in its A-frame taproom, Highside Brewing, Bagalis, Peppino's Pizza & Subs, Ein Prosit (a real beer hall hidden inside a Main Street antique store), and Frisco Adventure Park.

Dillon

Built on the shore of Dillon Reservoir after the original town was flooded to make it. Home to the highest deepwater marina in North America, a lakeside amphitheater with free summer concerts, and the best water views in the county. Sleepy after dark, which some people want. Go to Dillon Dam Brewery, the STEEP Brewing Lakeside Tiki Bar on the water in summer, Saved by the Wine, Red Mountain Grill, and Sapphire Point Overlook at sunset.

Silverthorne

First exit off I-70 and the most practical town in the county. Outlet shopping, a riverside food hall, a whitewater park, and Gold Medal trout water running right through the middle of town. Lodging and groceries cost less here than anywhere else in Summit County. Eat and drink at Bluebird Market, Sauce on the Blue, Mint (cook your own steak on a 700-degree stone), Timberline Craft Kitchen & Cocktails, Old Dillon Inn, and Angry James Brewery on the river.

Keystone

A resort-run village rather than a town, and it is built for families. Three mountains, the longest night-skiing operation in Colorado, free Kidtopia programming, and the largest Zamboni-maintained outdoor ice rink in North America. Arapahoe Basin and the ghost town of Montezuma are both a short drive up the road. Dining is the surprise here: Ski Tip Lodge (1800s stagecoach stop turned four-course inn), Alpenglow Stube (two gondolas up to 11,444 feet), Der Fondue Chessel, Kickapoo Tavern, and Snake River Saloon.

Copper Mountain

A purpose-built village at the base of a mountain that sorts itself: beginners west, intermediates center, experts east. Everything is walkable from the lifts, crowds are thinner than Breckenridge, and evenings are calm. Ten Mile Tavern, Jack's Slopeside Grill, Downhill Duke's, Sawmill Pizza & Taphouse, and Mulligan's Irish Pub cover the eating and drinking. Woodward Barn keeps kids busy the second the lifts close.

Summit County Group Trip Planning

Bachelorette weekends, bachelor parties, ski-buddy reunions, and multi-family condo trips all run on the same playbook here. Big groups work in Summit County because the lodging is condo-shaped and the towns are 10 to 20 minutes apart. Here is the complete version:

  • Stay: Breckenridge for walkable nightlife, Frisco if you want to be central and spend less, Silverthorne for the cheapest beds and easiest parking, Keystone or Copper Mountain if ski-in ski-out matters more than bars.
  • Get gear delivered: Ski Butlers and Black Tie Ski Rentals fit your entire group at the condo the night before, then pick everything up at the end. Christy Sports is the cheaper in-town option. Book 2 to 3 weeks out for holiday weeks.
  • Altitude recovery: Rocky Mountain IV Medics come to your condo, usually within an hour. Elite IV Lounge Breckenridge and PS Oxygen Bar are walk-in. Colorado Mountain Oxygen and High Country Oxygen deliver a concentrator if someone cannot sleep.
  • Group dinners that actually seat 10: Der Fondue Chessel and Alpenglow Stube at Keystone (gondola access, book ahead), Mint in Silverthorne, Dillon Dam Brewery, Bluebird Market, and Kemosabe at Silverheels in Frisco.
  • Non-ski day activities: snowmobiling and dog sledding with Good Times Adventures, dinner sleigh rides with Two Below Zero, a free tour at the Breckenridge Distillery, tubing at Frisco Adventure Park, or bowling and a movie at Foundry Cinema + Bowl.
  • Photo spots: Sapphire Point Overlook, the Blue River Plaza footbridge on Main Street, Dillon Marina at sunset, the BreckConnect gondola at golden hour, and Loveland Pass at 11,990 feet.
  • Cheap recovery: day passes at the Breckenridge Recreation Center or Silverthorne Recreation Center get you a hot tub, steam room, and sauna for less than a cocktail.

Skiing and Riding the Five Resorts

Five ski areas sit within about 30 minutes of each other, which is the whole reason to come here:

  • Breckenridge. Five peaks and the most terrain in the county. Imperial Express is the highest chairlift in North America at 12,840 feet, and everything above it is open bowl. Free gondola from town.
  • Keystone. Three mountains, huge beginner and intermediate acreage, and the longest night-skiing operation in Colorado. The family default.
  • Copper Mountain. Naturally sorted terrain, thinner lift lines, and the Woodward Barn for kids and park riders.
  • Arapahoe Basin. Base at 10,780 feet, steep East Wall terrain, and a season that regularly runs into June. Not a family cruiser, and locals love it for that.
  • Loveland. Just over the Continental Divide at the Eisenhower Tunnel. Cheapest lift ticket of the five, opens early, and almost no village to speak of.

Passes: Breckenridge and Keystone are on Epic, Copper Mountain and Arapahoe Basin are on Ikon, and Loveland sells its own. Buy any of them online well before you arrive, never at the window.

Want off the resort? Breckenridge Nordic Center and Frisco Adventure Park have groomed cross-country and snowshoe trails, and Colorado Adventure Guides runs guided backcountry days and avalanche courses. In summer the same terrain turns into hiking and mountain biking, with roughly 60 miles of paved recreation path connecting every town.

Family-Friendly Summit County

This whole county is built for families. Free or cheap kid-friendly activities:

  • Frisco Adventure Park. Tubing hill with a lift back to the top, a beginner ski hill, a bike park in summer, and a Nordic center. Town-run, so cheaper than the resorts.
  • Keystone Kidtopia. Free daily programming at the top of Dercum Mountain: a giant snow fort, parades, face painting, and fireworks on Saturdays in peak season.
  • Keystone Lake Ice Rink. The largest Zamboni-maintained outdoor rink in North America, with skate rentals on site.
  • Breckenridge Fun Park at Peak 8. Summer alpine slide, alpine coaster, gold panning, mini golf, and a climbing wall at the top of the free gondola.
  • Country Boy Mine (Breckenridge). Walk 1,000 feet into an 1887 gold mine, feed the donkeys, and ride a 55-foot ore-cart slide. Open year-round.
  • Carter Park sledding hill (Breckenridge). Free. Bring your own sled and expect a crowd after school.
  • Breckenridge and Silverthorne Recreation Centers. Pools with slides, climbing walls, and day passes for visitors. The best storm-day plan in the county.
  • Frisco Historic Park & Museum and the Edwin Carter Discovery Center. Both free, both about 30 minutes, both actually good.
  • Most Summit County taprooms are kid-friendly until early evening. Dillon Dam Brewery, Broken Compass, and Outer Range all seat families without a second thought.

Breweries and Distilleries

Colorado beer country, at 9,000 feet. The county's headliners are Outer Range Brewing Co. in Frisco (hazy IPAs and Belgian styles in a striking A-frame taproom), Dillon Dam Brewery (one of the largest brewpubs in Colorado, open since 1997), and Breckenridge Brewery & Pub, the original 1990 South Main brewpub where Avalanche Ale started. Add Broken Compass Brewing (coconut porter, a chairlift for a bar bench, food trucks most days), Highside Brewing in Frisco, Steep Brewing & Coffee Co. and Syndicate Brewing Co. in Breckenridge, and Angry James Brewery on the Blue River in Silverthorne.

For spirits, the Breckenridge Distillery on Airport Road calls itself the World's Highest Distillery and runs free guided tours, paid tastings, and a full restaurant. Mythology Distillery pours whiskey and rum in a historic barn on North Main, and Continental Divide Winery makes wine at 9,600 feet on South Main. Day trip further for 10th Mountain Whiskey & Spirit Co. in Vail (45 min west), Golden Moon Distillery in Golden (75 min east), or Stranahan's in Denver (90 min east).

Waterfalls and Alpine Lakes Near Summit County

Colorado has stunning waterfalls and alpine lakes within day-trip distance, and the first several are barely a drive at all:

  • Continental Falls (Breckenridge, 20 min). Roaring cascade on the Spruce Creek Trail. About 2.5 miles round trip. Peak flow is late June into July.
  • Mohawk Lakes (Breckenridge, 20 min). Continue past Continental Falls to a chain of alpine lakes with old mining cabins and a preserved tramway. Roughly 6.5 miles round trip and one of the best hikes in the county.
  • McCullough Gulch Falls (Breckenridge, 25 min). Waterfall, alpine lake, and miners' cabins below Quandary Peak. Parking is tiny. Arrive before 7am on summer weekends or take the shuttle.
  • Blue Lakes and Monte Cristo Creek (Breckenridge, 25 min). Drive-up waterfall pouring off the dam below Quandary Peak. Almost no hiking required, and moose are common in the willows.
  • Lower Cataract Lake and Cataract Falls (45 min north of Silverthorne). Easy 2.5-mile lake loop in the Eagles Nest Wilderness. Wildflowers in July, gold aspens in September. Small day-use fee.
  • Booth Falls (Vail, 45 min west). A 60-foot waterfall two miles up the Booth Creek Trail. Steep but straightforward, and paid parking fills early.
  • Hanging Lake (Glenwood Canyon, 2 hours west). Turquoise travertine lake fed by Bridal Veil Falls. Permit and shuttle reservation required, and they sell out weeks ahead.
  • Rifle Falls State Park (2.5 hours west). Triple waterfall over a limestone wall with caves you can walk into behind it. Flat trails, great with kids. State parks pass required.

Getting Around Summit County Without a Car

The Summit Stage is a free county-wide bus connecting Breckenridge, Frisco, Dillon, Silverthorne, Keystone, and Copper Mountain every day of the year, with ski racks on the front and no fare or pass of any kind. Each town layers its own free shuttle on top of it: the Breckenridge Free Ride, the Frisco Flyer, and the Keystone and Copper village routes. Between the two systems you can run a whole ski week without ever starting a car. Uber and Lyft both operate here, but driver supply thins out late at night and surge pricing at closing time is real, so plan the last ride before you need it.

Nearly everyone flies into Denver International Airport (DEN) and drives I-70 west, which takes 75 to 100 minutes without weather. Shared shuttles like Epic Mountain Express run door to door if you would rather not drive. In winter, rent something with all-wheel drive and snow-rated tires, check cotrip.org before you leave, and expect the Colorado traction law to be in effect on I-70 during storms. Avoid heading back east on Sunday afternoons if you can help it.

Local Tips Before You Land

  • You are at 9,000 to 10,000 feet. Drink 2 to 3 liters of water a day, plan an easy first day, and skip the hard hike until day three.
  • Alcohol hits about twice as hard up here. Take it easy the first night and you will avoid most of what people call altitude sickness.
  • Sunscreen even on cloudy days. Thin air, high UV, and snow reflecting it back at you.
  • Book dinner weeks out in ski season. Hearthstone, Briar Rose, Ski Tip Lodge, Rootstalk, Der Fondue Chessel, and Alpenglow Stube all fill early.
  • Book gear delivery and snow tours 2 to 3 weeks ahead for holiday weeks. They sell out.
  • Mud season is real. Late April into May and much of October, plenty of restaurants close for a few weeks. Call before you drive.
  • Park free in the F-Lot in Breckenridge and ride the free gondola in rather than circling Main Street.
  • Summer trailhead lots fill before 7am. McCullough Gulch and Quandary Peak both use a shuttle in peak season.
  • The Summit Stage is free. No ticket, no app, no pass. Most visitors never figure this out.
