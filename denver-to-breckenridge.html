Denver to Breckenridge

Howdy Summit · Drive times, shuttles, and I-70 · Updated 2026

Eighty miles separate Denver International Airport from Breckenridge, and on a clear Tuesday you will cover them in under two hours. On the Saturday of a holiday weekend, in snow, the same drive can take five. The difference is almost entirely about when you travel and whether I-70 is behaving.

This page covers what that drive actually looks like, the alternatives if you would rather not do it yourself, and what to do when the Eisenhower Tunnel shuts.

Real Drive Times, by Day and Season

Mapping apps will tell you 1 hour 45 minutes. That number is true at 10am on a Wednesday in September. It is not true when you need it most.

  • Weekday, off-season: 1h45m to 2h. The honest baseline.
  • Saturday morning westbound, ski season: 2h30m to 3h30m. Everyone in Denver leaves between 6am and 9am. This is the single worst window.
  • Sunday afternoon eastbound, ski season: 3h to 4h. The return is reliably worse than the outbound, because the whole Front Range drives home at once.
  • During or after a storm: unbounded. A single crash in the tunnel can hold traffic for three hours.
  • Summer weekend: 2h to 2h30m. Better than winter, but the mountain corridor is busy year round now.

The local move is simple: leave before 6am or after 11am going west, and before noon or after 6pm coming back. An hour of patience at either end routinely saves two on the road.

The Route, Turn by Turn

There is essentially one way, and it is straightforward:

  • DIA to I-70: Pena Boulevard south, then I-70 west. Roughly 30 minutes just to clear Denver.
  • Denver to Idaho Springs: the climb begins. You gain 2,000 feet without really noticing.
  • Floyd Hill: around mile marker 247. A long-running construction zone and a reliable slowdown.
  • Georgetown to Silver Plume: the steepest sustained grade. Watch your engine temperature in summer.
  • Eisenhower Tunnel: mile 213, elevation 11,158 feet. The highest point on the entire US Interstate system.
  • Exit 203, CO-9: ten miles south to Breckenridge past the Dillon Reservoir.

The tunnel is the pinch point for the whole corridor. Everything upstream of it backs up when it slows.

Shuttles and Buses from DIA

Plenty of visitors skip the drive entirely, and in winter that is often the smarter call.

  • Epic Mountain Express: the dominant shared shuttle, DIA direct to Breckenridge lodging. Book ahead in ski season.
  • Summit Express: shared and private options on the same route.
  • Peak 1 Express: another established local operator.
  • Private SUV transfers: more expensive, but worth it for groups with a lot of gear, and they handle snow better than a rental sedan.

Shuttles take longer than driving yourself because of stops, typically 2.5 to 3 hours. What you get in exchange is somebody else dealing with the traction law, the tunnel, and parking.

Prices move around, so confirm at booking rather than trusting a number on any page including this one.

When I-70 Closes

This is the part most guides skip, and it is the part that ruins trips.

I-70 through the Eisenhower Tunnel closes several times each winter, for crashes, avalanche control, or simply too much snow. When it does, CDOT typically diverts traffic onto US-6 over Loveland Pass, which climbs to 11,990 feet on switchbacks with no guardrail in places. It is slower, more exposed, and closed to vehicles carrying hazardous materials.

In a serious storm Loveland Pass closes as well. At that point there is no westbound route through the corridor and the honest answer is that you wait, either in Denver or in Silverthorne depending on which side you are stuck on.

What to do:

  • Check before you leave, not on the way. Once you are past Georgetown your options narrow considerably.
  • Keep water, snacks and a warm layer in the car between November and April. Sitting stationary for three hours at 11,000 feet is a genuinely different experience without them.
  • Do not trust a mapping app's ETA during a closure. They reroute optimistically and will happily send you over a pass that is also shut.

The Howdy Summit app pulls live CDOT data and shows an alert the moment I-70, CO-9, Loveland Pass or Fremont Pass closes, so you can check in a second before setting off. You can also read conditions directly at COtrip.org or by calling 511 in Colorado.

Winter Driving and the Traction Law

From roughly September through May, Colorado enforces a traction law on I-70 in the mountain corridor. Your vehicle must have one of:

  • Four-wheel or all-wheel drive with adequate tread
  • Snow tires, or tires rated mud and snow
  • Chains or an approved traction device

Standard rental sedans on summer tires frequently do not qualify, and the fine for being caught out is real, more so if you block the road. If you are renting in winter, ask specifically for all-wheel drive and confirm the tires, do not assume an SUV badge means anything.

Two more things worth knowing. Your tank empties faster at altitude in cold weather, so fill up in Denver or Silverthorne rather than gambling. And the drive back east is downhill for twenty continuous miles, which cooks brakes; use a lower gear rather than riding them.

Do You Need a Car Once You Arrive?

Genuinely, no. Summit County has better free transit than most American cities.

  • The Summit Stage is free and connects Breckenridge, Frisco, Dillon, Silverthorne, Keystone and Copper Mountain. No ticket, no app, no pass. Most visitors never discover this.
  • The Breckenridge Free Ride shuttle loops the town and the ski base.
  • The BreckConnect gondola runs from the free F-Lot to Peak 8, and is free.

A car helps for early trailhead starts and spontaneous resort-hopping. For a straightforward ski trip staying in town, a shuttle in and the free buses locally works well and removes parking from the equation entirely.

Common Questions

How long is the drive really? Eighty miles, 1h45m clear, 2.5 to 3 hours on a winter weekend, longer during storms.

Is it a scary drive? It is a maintained interstate the whole way, not a mountain track. But it reaches 11,158 feet, weather changes fast, and it deserves respect in winter.

Can I do it at night? Yes, and traffic is far lighter. Wildlife is more active, and snowplows work overnight.

What about the return to catch a flight? Give yourself four hours from Breckenridge to DIA in winter. People miss flights on this drive every single season.
